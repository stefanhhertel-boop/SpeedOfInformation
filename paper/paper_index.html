Abstract
For over a century, the fundamental kinematic invariant of the universe, \(c \approx 2.99792458 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}\), has carried the historical nomenclature "the speed of light in vacuum." While historically understandable given the derivation of Maxwell's electrodynamics, this terminology has fostered persistent pedagogical confusions, obscured the axiomatic structure of spacetime, and detached the kinematic foundation of relativity from modern developments in quantum information theory, many-body physics, and holographic quantum gravity. In this study, we conduct an extensive theoretical and computational investigation into whether replacing the concept of the "speed of light" (\(c\)) with the "speed of information" (\(c_{\mathcal{I}}\)) represents a substantive, major step forward in theoretical physics. We prove via group-theoretic and causal order methods that spacetime geometry requires only an invariant causal velocity \(c_{\mathcal{I}}\) decoupled from any gauge field or massless particle; demonstrate via Kramers-Kronig analyticity that superluminal group velocities in media are envelope reshaping artifacts; show how Lieb-Robinson bounds generate emergent light cones in non-relativistic matter; and evaluate how holographic butterfly velocities position information flow as the precursor to spacetime metric structures.

1. Introduction: The Historical Accident of Maxwellian Relativism

In 1905, Albert Einstein published his landmark paper establishing Special Relativity based upon two postulates: the Principle of Relativity and the Constancy of the Speed of Light. While Maxwell's equations accurately identified the propagation speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum as \(c = 1/\sqrt{\epsilon_0 \mu_0}\), embedding the word "light" into the foundation of spacetime physics has created persistent ontological vulnerabilities.

Students and researchers frequently grapple with basic questions: Why does relativity hold in media where light travels slower than \(c\)? What happens if photons have a tiny non-zero rest mass? Why do apparent "superluminal laser pulses" not violate causality? In modern physics, spacetime is understood to emerge from quantum information (the "It from Qubit" paradigm). The historical baggage of "speed of light" obscures this fundamental relationship.

\[ x' = \frac{x - vt}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c_{\mathcal{I}}^2}}, \quad t' = \frac{t - v x / c_{\mathcal{I}}^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c_{\mathcal{I}}^2}}. \]

Not a single photon or electromagnetic field is required to define this speed! In de Broglie-Proca electrodynamics, where the photon carries a tiny rest mass \(m_\gamma\), the group velocity of light becomes dispersive:

\[ [\hat{\mathcal{O}}_A(x), \hat{\mathcal{O}}_B(y)] = 0 \quad \text{for } (x - y)^2 < 0. \]
